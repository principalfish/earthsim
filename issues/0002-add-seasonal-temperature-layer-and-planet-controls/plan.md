## Context

earth_sim is a browser-based planet simulator for worldbuilding. Issue 0001 built the foundation: a cubed-sphere grid, a world of elevations, a Three.js globe, sculpting with undo, a Web Worker returning terrain statistics, a new-world dialog, saving, and an Earth preset. This issue is roadmap step 2, the first simulated layer: near-surface air temperature through the year, with the planet controls it needs, its display on the globe, and a comparison against observed Earth. Believable results and a fast simulation matter more than fidelity.

**This issue is built from `main` after issue 0001 is merged.** Do not start before that.

### Decisions (all settled with the user on 2026-11-11 unless dated otherwise)

- **Scope:** the temperature model, its display on the globe, planet controls, and an in-app comparison with observed Earth. Not included: orbit eccentricity, rotation rate (a monthly energy balance does not depend on day length), a flat map view, winds, currents, rainfall, real ice sheets.
- **Model:** a seasonal energy balance on the world's own grid: steps through the year, repeated until they settle, with sunlight by latitude and season, land warming and cooling faster than ocean, heat spreading between neighbouring cells, cooling with height, and snow or ice brightening cold surfaces by a simple temperature rule that the later ice step replaces. Output: twelve monthly fields of near-surface air temperature in degrees Celsius, one value per cell.
- **Planet controls:** axial tilt 0 to 90 degrees (default 23.44); star brightness 0.1 to 10 times the Sun (default 1); star mass 0.1 to 10 times the Sun (default 1); orbit distance 0.3 to 3 Earth-Sun distances (default 1); greenhouse strength relative to Earth, 0 (none) to 3 (default 1). Read-only lines show sunlight received compared with Earth (from brightness and distance) and the year's length (from mass and distance). Star mass is its own control by the user's choice ("Add star mass as a setting"), so any brightness can be paired with any mass.
- **Year length matters to the model.** The user asked whether it does. A season's length in real time sets how far land and ocean warm and cool before the sun moves on: a short year blurs the seasons and a long year deepens them. The solver must therefore step in real time derived from the year length, not in abstract month units. A note appears when sunlight received is far from Earth's, saying results there are rough; where it starts is settled from the first runs.
- **Recalculation:** automatic after edits. How long a solve takes at 64, 128 and 256 is measured first; the user then decides the exact trigger (only while the temperature layer is showing, or always). See the stop after piece 4.
- **Display:** temperature replaces the height colours while relief stays raised and coastlines stay outlined. A month slider with play; annual summaries (annual average, warmest month, coldest month, seasonal range); a legend; the value under the cursor.
- **Colour scale:** fitted to the world on screen, not fixed (the user chose this over a fixed scale). It spans the whole year for that world, so a colour means the same temperature in every month and the month animation does not rescale.
- **Date:** the page shows the world's date, counted in years since the world began: zero when a world is created (the Earth preset's zero means today), saved with the world, shown with the month, such as "Year 0 · July". Nothing advances the year in this issue; later steps do, and it then reads as, for example, "12,000 years" or "2.4 million years".
- **Months:** January to December on every world, twelve equal parts of the year whatever its length, with northern midsummer in late June as on Earth.
- **Earth comparison:** when the world started as the Earth preset, the layer can show simulated, observed, or the difference, with summary error figures. Built so each later step adds its own observed layer. The same data drives automated tests.
- **Observed data:** NOAA's 20th Century Reanalysis version 3, 2 m air temperature long-term monthly means, 1981 to 2010, 1 degree grid, global. Source: `https://downloads.psl.noaa.gov/Datasets/20thC_ReanV3/Derived/Monthlies/2mSI-MO/air.2m.mon.ltm.nc` (4.5 MB, netCDF-3 classic, no login). Terms as read on NOAA PSL's page for the dataset on 2026-11-11: "not subject to copyright protection in the United States. NOAA waives any potential copyright ... through the Creative Commons Zero 1.0 Universal Public Domain Dedication (CC0-1.0)". A small derived file is committed; the download is not.
- **The sun in the scene** (added after the builder started; "as part of this can we render the sun in the background when appropriate"): the star is always drawn, and shows whenever it is in view and not hidden by the planet. It sits over one fixed line of longitude and moves north and south with the month on screen as the tilt dictates; the model has no time of day. Its apparent size and colour follow the star settings, drawn larger than life with a glow. A Sunlight toggle, off by default, lights the globe from it with a day side and a night side; off, the globe stays evenly lit as now.
- **Good enough:** the right broad pattern, not numeric targets. With no winds or currents the simulated Earth will not match closely. The error against observed Earth is measured and stored as a baseline that later steps must not worsen.
- **Carried in from issue 0001's self review:** `startStatistics` in `src/app/main.ts` holds an untested decision (no worker, no client, a worker error event) and a `throw` that can never run. Piece 5 moves that decision into tested code.
- **Carried over from issue 0001:** the simulation is a pure TypeScript library (`src/core`) with no browser dependency, run in a Web Worker as a compute service; the page owns the editable world and the worker keeps no state between requests; everything is client-side static files; Biome owns formatting; a run through time must later be pauseable with the map editable while paused.

## Approach

**Planet settings and date live on the world.** A `planet` object (tilt, star brightness, orbit distance, greenhouse) and an `ageYears` number are added to `World`, so they travel to the worker with it and are saved with it.

**Sunlight is exact; the rest is tuned.** Daily-mean sunlight at the top of the atmosphere, by latitude and time of year, has a closed form for any tilt on a circular orbit, including polar day and night. Monthly values are means over each twelfth of the year. Year length follows exactly from Kepler's third law: in Earth years, the square root of orbit distance cubed over star mass, both in solar units.

**The solver is a diffusive energy balance.** For each cell, heat capacity times the rate of temperature change equals absorbed sunlight, minus outgoing heat (linear in temperature, scaled by greenhouse strength), plus heat exchanged with the four neighbours. The neighbour exchange must be written as fluxes across cell edges so that energy is conserved exactly; this needs edge lengths and centre spacings the grid does not yet provide. Ocean cells get a mixed-layer heat capacity, land cells a much smaller one. The balance is solved for sea-level-equivalent temperature and land is then cooled by a fixed lapse rate per kilometre above sea level. Reflectivity depends on surface type and rises as a cell gets cold. Internal time steps may be shorter than a month for stability; output is twelve monthly means. The run repeats the year until the annual-mean field stops changing, or a cap is reached, and reports which. A request may carry a previous result as a starting state, so a re-solve after a small edit converges in fewer years; the worker stays stateless. Constants start from published simple-model values and are tuned against the observed Earth; they are tuning parameters, not physical truth.

**A second worker instance for temperature.** The same worker script, a second instance, with a new request kind. When a newer request supersedes a solve still running, the page terminates that worker and starts a fresh one, so the newest request never queues behind a stale one. Results are transferred.

**One display layer at a time.** The page holds the twelve monthly fields and derives the summaries from them on demand. Only the field on screen is uploaded, as a second cube texture laid out like the elevation one; the shader's sampling functions take the sampler as a parameter so both textures share the edge blending. The colour ramp and its range are uniforms (or a small ramp texture), not baked in.

**Observed data on its own grid.** The committed file keeps the source's 1 degree latitude-longitude grid, twelve months, one byte per value in half-degree steps (about 0.8 MB); `src/core` regrids it to the world's cells when needed.

**Rejected alternatives.**
- A fixed colour scale: recommended to the user, who chose a fitted one.
- Deriving the star's mass from its brightness (recommended), or treating every year as Earth-length: the user chose star mass as its own control.
- Numeric accuracy targets now, or tuning by eye alone: the user chose pattern tests plus a recorded baseline.
- NCEP-DOE Reanalysis 2 (coarser, terms a usage note not a named licence) and ERA5 (needs an account and thirty years averaged by hand).
- Saving computed layers in the world file: needs a format version change; recalculating on load is simpler.
- Running temperature on the statistics worker: a slow solve would freeze the statistics readout.
- Committing the observed data already regridded to 256 per face: about 9 MB for twelve months.
