# feat: move layers and overlays into the main view

The controls for what the globe shows lived in two side-panel sections: Layers for the colour layer, its view of the year and the month, and View for the winds, sunlight and cell grid. More layers are coming (rainfall, ocean currents, ice, storms, biomes), so this moves those controls onto the globe's own area, one click from what you're looking at, and adds units.

**The main view.** Icon tiles, drawn for the project in code, run down the left edge: Terrain, Temperature and Pressure as one choice, then Winds, Sunlight and Grid as toggles. In a narrow window they show icons only and the dock scrolls. With Temperature or Pressure showing, a top bar holds the layer's Show (month, annual average or seasonal range) and, on an untouched Earth, one Simulated, Observed or Difference control that now applies to the layer and the winds together. A month timeline with Play runs along the bottom, and the side panel's Layers section is gone. The legend, one status line and the value under the pointer are one info box in the corner. The pointer reads in two short lines ("1.7° N, 49.1° E · 4,628 m deep" / "28 °C · 15 mph S"). The wind key, the legend's end labels and the wind comparison figures were dropped along the way, as not earning their place.

**Always worked out, with Pause.** The climate is solved after every change to the world and when the page opens, whatever is on screen, since most of the page depends on it. A Pause button in the corner stops new solves while sculpting: a waiting request is cancelled, a solve under way finishes, and releasing it catches up once. The status covers the whole solve ("Calculating temperature", then "Calculating winds", or "Paused"). Pause isn't remembered.

**Remembered, and Settings.** The browser now remembers the layer, each layer's Show and the three toggles; never the month, the comparison or Pause. A Settings section, last in the side panel, holds how winds are drawn, which now starts on streaks (arrows when the system asks for reduced motion), and Units: Metric or Imperial, with an override per quantity (temperature with K, pressure, wind speed with km/h and knots, heights and depths, distances). Units reach every read-out and control: the legend, refitted so its ticks are round in the chosen unit; the pointer; Statistics; Sea level; the brush; and the New world dialog's cell sizes. Sliders keep their metric steps underneath. Choosing Metric or Imperial, even the one already chosen, clears every override. A previously stored wind unit carries over as the wind override. The user guide is reorganised around the main view.

**Review.** Each of the 12 pieces had its own review round and light re-check; a manual review was done by hand for the tiles, the top bar, the timeline, the info box, Pause, and units in the read-outs and controls. The self review of the whole branch found one regression that only shows across pieces: the controls moved over the globe stayed usable while the saved world was loading (on `main` they sat inside the panel, which is locked then), so Pause then Resume could start a wasted solve of the blank world. That is fixed, with five small doc and test cleanups, in the last commit. Nothing was skipped.

**Worth checking by hand:**
- The Metric/Imperial switch's keyboard behaviour (Space and the arrow keys) was tested in Chromium-based Edge only; Firefox wasn't tried.
- Switching units can move a legend's ends slightly, so the globe's colours may shift a little; in Metric they are exactly as before.
- Frame times were measured in headless Edge, which draws in software, so they compare cases rather than show real GPU speed.

Not included: any change to the temperature or wind models, a phone-sized layout, heat carried by winds, and the folder refactor that follows. Commit titles for pieces 2–10 say "/11" because a twelfth piece (the Metric/Imperial reset) was added at piece 11; piece 1's title predates the conventional-commit format.
